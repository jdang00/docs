---
title: "What to do after a missed question"
description: "Turn a wrong answer, uncertain guess, or free-response correction into a useful next attempt."
---

The useful part of a missed question is finding *which decision failed*. Make the correction while the question is still in front of you, then return to it later without the answer visible.

## Diagnose the miss

Ask yourself which of these happened:

| What happened | What to do next |
| --- | --- |
| You did not know the fact or term | Read the rationale and the cited source page, then restate the core fact from memory. |
| You knew the topic but chose a tempting option | Name the finding or qualifier that rules out your choice. |
| You misread the stem or finding | Point to the word, number, or condition that changes the answer. Slow down on similar stems. |
| You had the right conclusion but could not explain it | Rebuild the reasoning in one or two sentences before checking the reference answer again. |
| You understood the topic but ran out of time | Practice a smaller timed set and separate pacing from content gaps. |

The goal is a correction you can use on the next question, such as “I confused a test condition with its result,” rather than “study this more.”

## Use the module tools

1. After **Check** or **Submit**, read the feedback and reveal the rationale when available. For free response, open **More** to compare your response with the ground truth.
2. Try the question again after correcting the missing idea. For free response, rewrite the point in your own words and submit it.
3. Use **Flag** if you guessed, still feel uncertain, or want to revisit the item. Flags also work for questions you got right for the wrong reason.
4. Open **Notes** in the module **Tools** area and save a short, question-specific correction: what misled you, what distinguishes the right answer, and what to check next time. Notes are private and save automatically.
5. Come back in a later module run and answer before looking at the solution.

Use the module's flagged or unanswered filters to plan another pass. A checked wrong answer can still count toward module completion, so look at the correctness and mastery signals as well. See [What progress and mastery mean](/students/understanding-progress-and-mastery).

## After a custom test

Open **Review** on the results page. Start with **Incorrect**, then check **Flagged** for uncertain correct answers and **Unanswered** for skipped or timed-out items. Identify a pattern across items before building another test. A new **Flagged** custom test uses questions flagged in module practice; you can also choose specific modules to revisit the concepts you missed. Free-response questions stay in module practice.
