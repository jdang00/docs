---
title: "Reading question analytics in a small cohort"
description: "Use exposure, flags, and mastery to choose which questions deserve a closer editorial review."
---

In a small cohort, a few students can change a percentage sharply. Start with counts and the question itself. The **Class Progress** workspace helps you locate questions to inspect; it does not show a question-level error rate or tell you why someone stopped studying.

## Start with the denominator

Open a module's **Questions** detail. For each item, note how many students tried it and how many did not. The view also shows student flags and last interaction. A low interaction rate can mean the question appears late in a module, the module is new, few students have started, or the item is being skipped. It does not establish that the item is difficult.

Module **Question coverage** counts tried student–question pairs divided by all possible pairs. **Students studying** counts people who tried at least one question in the module. Those measures answer different questions: reach within the module versus participation in it. See [Reading progress analytics](/admin/reading-progress-analytics) for definitions.

## Compare signals before editing

| Pattern | First check | Possible response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Few students tried the item | Module timing, question order, and overall participation | Wait for more exposure or make the module easier to find. |
| Several flags among students who tried it | Stem, options, rationale, source page, and learner view | Clarify wording or correct the item if you find a concrete defect. |
| Low mastery with meaningful exposure | Whether students have had completed runs separated in time | Allow spaced review; inspect the item for ambiguity if concerns persist. |
| One student flagged it | Their flag as an investigation lead | Read the item and source before changing a shared bank. |
| Many items have low exposure | Module length and where students tend to stop | Review module organization before rewriting individual questions. |

The **Flags** tab lists a limited group of the most flagged questions; it is a shortlist, not a full inventory. The **Activity** tab shows a limited recent sample. Use **Questions** and pagination to inspect the full module. The available question view reports interaction, flags, and mastery. It does not expose response choices or a per-question correctness percentage, so do not interpret a low mastery rate as an item failure rate.

## Use a decision sequence

1. Pick a specific item and record its tried count, flag count, and last interaction. Compare it with nearby questions in the same module.
2. Check whether enough students encountered it to support a pattern. In a small cohort, describe the counts in plain language rather than treating a percentage as precise.
3. Open the question in curation. Answer from the stem, inspect the correct answer and rationale, and verify the cited source or course material.
4. If you find a defect, edit or move the item to **Draft** while repairing it. If the item is sound, watch for more exposure or ask for specific learner feedback through your normal course channel.
5. After a change, recheck the learner view and record what you changed so later reviewers can interpret the trend.
