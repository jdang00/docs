## Why this history matters

LearnTerms makes more sense when you know what it was built to solve first.

The product started as a response to a real class workflow, then expanded in layers:

- first around term study
- then around efficient high-yield filtering
- then around AI-assisted authoring and curation
- then around shared cohort structure, analytics, badges, and custom tests
- and more recently around source-backed authoring, written answers, and clearer study feedback

That progression explains why LearnTerms feels opinionated today.

## The short version

LearnTerms began as a focused tool for mastering course terms. The [project README](https://github.com/jdang00/learnterms#why-learnterms) traces that need to weekly medical-terminology quizzes in NSUOCO's Introduction to Optometry course: long word lists, exact spelling, and combining forms. Over time, LearnTerms grew into a cohort-based platform organized by **semester**, **class**, **module**, and **question**.

The current product reflects several big transitions:

1. from solo studying to shared cohort studying
2. from term lists to module-based question banks
3. from manual authoring alone to AI-assisted generation plus human curation
4. from simple practice loops to richer progress, badges, and custom tests
5. from adding questions quickly to checking their evidence, explanations, and learner experience

## Early foundation: LearnTerms 1.0

The archived **LearnTerms 1.0 Release** post dates the first major public version to **September 22, 2024** in the article body. The blog index and [GitHub release](https://github.com/jdang00/learnterms/releases/tag/releases) use **September 23, 2024**. The first repository commit was [September 3, 2024](https://github.com/jdang00/learnterms/commit/47b0f4e8e2f71d64ee2fe7fbe410f3bf78e57604).

At that stage, the product was still much narrower than it is today. The focus was:

- learning course terms accurately
- reinforcing recall by typing
- reviewing starred or missed content
- using a lightweight study tool without heavy setup

The original framing was very direct: LearnTerms existed to help a specific class do better on quizzes with less friction.

## October 2024: the LENS era

The archived **LENS Algorithm** post is dated **October 16, 2024**.

This is one of the clearest windows into the product philosophy. Instead of assuming that "more cards" automatically means "better studying," LearnTerms explored a more selective model. The LENS system focused on cutting low-value content and keeping high-yield material.

That idea still shows up in the product today. LearnTerms is not trying to be an everything bucket. It is trying to help you study the right material with less waste.

## Early 2025: LearnTerms v2

The archive index lists:

- **LearnTerms v2 Beta** on **January 15, 2025**
- **LearnTerms v2** on **February 28, 2025**

The [GitHub v2 release](https://github.com/jdang00/learnterms/releases/tag/v2) is dated **March 1, 2025 UTC**. The archived article pages currently show **2024** in their headers, which conflicts with the archive index and release record.

Based on the archive index and the content of the posts, v2 marked a major shift:

- the product moved beyond simple term review
- exam-style study flow became a bigger focus
- AI-powered question generation entered the picture
- the platform started to look more like a durable class resource than a single-course tool

This was the point where LearnTerms began acting like a system, not just a utility.

## Mid to late 2025: infrastructure and content operations

The changelog shows **July 2025** through **December 2025** as the period where major platform foundations were laid.

Highlights from that stretch include:

- **July 2025**: foundational navigation and Convex migration work
- **August 2025**: content library, AI generation, image support, and broader authoring capabilities expanded
- **September 2025**: matching and editor refinements improved authoring quality
- **October 2025**: progress tracking became richer, including stronger support for fill in the blank and matching workflows
- **December 2025**: performance cleanup simplified progress handling and moved module question counts toward stored fields

This period matters because it turned LearnTerms into something operationally maintainable. The app was no longer only about the study screen. It had become a structured content system.

## January 2026: redesign and curation maturity

The changelog marks **January 2026** as a major redesign and curation month.

Key themes:

- student and admin interfaces were redesigned
- class management tooling expanded
- question curation workflows improved
- tagging groundwork landed
- progress and analytics became more visible

This is when LearnTerms started to feel much more like the current product. The admin side, contributor side, and student side were becoming parts of one coherent platform.

## February 2026: badges and a stronger shared experience

By **February 2026**, the changelog shows another layer being added:

- badges
- cohort page improvements
- visual redesign work
- stronger analytics
- more polished AI helper behavior

This was important because LearnTerms had always been cohort-oriented in structure, but now it became more cohort-visible in the interface too.

## March 2026: LearnTerms v3

The changelog names **March 2026** as the **LearnTerms v3** release.

The biggest visible shifts in that release window were:

- v3 itself shipping as the largest platform update to date
- custom tests becoming a first-class workflow
- more polished study ergonomics
- a fuller changelog and rollout layer

Custom tests are especially important in the product story. They show the platform moving beyond "study one module at a time" toward "build exam-style review from your own real class content."

## April and May 2026: better editing and study support

After v3, the next changes improved work people were already doing. [April's changelog entries](https://learnterms.com/changelog) cover richer question editing, clearer validation, matching-answer repairs, configurable class cards, and an updated grade calculator. [May's badge changes](https://github.com/jdang00/learnterms/pull/152) recognized larger quizzes and finals preparation, with a [new award display](https://github.com/jdang00/learnterms/pull/153).

These updates matter because a shared question bank depends on dependable editing. A contributor needs to know why an answer or format is invalid before a question reaches students. Students need the study screen and its feedback to behave predictably across question types.

## June and July 2026: organizing entry and generation

[June's join-class work](https://github.com/jdang00/learnterms/pull/154) brought account setup into the class-joining flow. [Cohort-managed quick links](https://github.com/jdang00/learnterms/commit/fcf3fe9) gave staff a way to organize course resources. Question Studio's [workflow redesign](https://github.com/jdang00/learnterms/commit/c794770) changed how contributors set up generation.

In July, Question Studio began [batching drafting turns](https://github.com/jdang00/learnterms/commit/b898238) and using [tool-first workers](https://github.com/jdang00/learnterms/commit/8810cf8) to retrieve and apply source material. These were steps toward a more inspectable document-to-question workflow; a generated draft still needed contributor review.

## September 2026: evidence, feedback, and curation

The [September changelog](https://learnterms.com/changelog) records the broadest post-v3 change to the current study and authoring flow. [Question Studio and Content Library updates](https://github.com/jdang00/learnterms/pull/155) brought document processing, source-page and topic mapping, evidence-backed candidates, editable drafts, and visible generation progress into one workflow. Contributors could also [attach source pages to manually edited questions](https://github.com/jdang00/learnterms/commit/4eee54d), making the answer easier to verify later.

On the student side, the [quiz dock, module completion view, and persistent highlights](https://github.com/jdang00/learnterms/commit/630155c) made practice easier to resume and review. [Mastery became a passive result of spaced, clean recall](https://github.com/jdang00/learnterms/commit/e3493c1), separate from simply checking every question. [Private question notes and a calculator](https://github.com/jdang00/learnterms/commit/4eee54d) added tools beside the study flow. [Free-response checking and mobile quiz changes](https://github.com/jdang00/learnterms/commit/b843173) expanded how students could answer and receive feedback.

For staff, [class progress](https://github.com/jdang00/learnterms/commit/ca52c07) and the [live curator dashboard](https://github.com/jdang00/learnterms/commit/1429b29) made participation, question coverage, flags, and module activity easier to inspect. Those signals help identify what deserves review; the question and its source still need a human check. Late-month `main` commits also added a [beta clinical exam-finding editor and learner display](https://github.com/jdang00/learnterms/commit/d01be80), followed by more [structured finding types](https://github.com/jdang00/learnterms/commit/3ea6984). The beta label matters: those commits document work in progress, not a new general-purpose clinical record.

The newer guides follow these changes. [Students](/students/exam-week-study-plan) can use feedback, flags, and mastery to plan review. [Contributors](/contributors/pre-publish-question-checklist) can check source support and answerability before publishing. [Admins](/admin/reading-small-cohort-question-analytics) can interpret limited exposure before changing a question bank.

## What stayed consistent across all versions

A lot changed, but a few ideas stayed surprisingly stable:

- LearnTerms is meant to be high yield, not exhaustive for its own sake.
- The product mirrors real coursework instead of forcing you to invent your own structure.
- Human judgment still matters, especially in question quality.
- The fastest workflow is not always the best workflow; the product tries to preserve useful focus.

## Why the current docs are structured this way

The external docs follow the current product shape, but the history explains why the sections exist:

- **Students** reflects the mature study flow
- **Admins** reflects the need for clean cohort and content operations
- **Contributors** reflects the reality that good question banks need active curation
- **Platform** reflects the fact that LearnTerms is now a real system with roles, routes, and data boundaries
