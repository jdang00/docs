---
title: "A question review checklist before publishing"
description: "Check source support, answerability, grading, and the learner view before a question enters study practice."
---

Keep a question as **Draft** until a reviewer can answer it and learn from its feedback. Use this checklist for manually written, generated, duplicated, and heavily edited questions.

## 1. Confirm the learning target

- Write the one thing the student should know or do after answering.
- Check that the question belongs in the selected module and matches its current course material.
- Remove details that do not change the answer or the intended reasoning.
- Check the format against the skill: recognition, short recall, paired relationships, or explanation. See [Choosing the right question type](/contributors/choosing-question-types).

## 2. Verify the answer against the source

- Open the relevant course document or other approved source. Do not rely on a generated candidate's citation alone.
- Confirm the stated answer, terminology, and any clinical thresholds or recommendations.
- For a manual citation, choose only pages that directly support the rationale. Preview the original page, then save the citation. See [Manual source citations](/contributors/manual-source-citations).
- If the source cannot support the question as written, narrow the claim or leave the item in **Draft**.

## 3. Check answerability and grading

- Read the stem without the answer or rationale. Check that it names one task and supplies the needed case details.
- For multiple choice, check that every option is distinct and that the marked answer is defensible. For matching, check every pair. For fill in the blank, check accepted wording and units.
- For free response, ensure the **Rationale** covers each requested point and the **Acceptance** level fits the prompt. See [Handling alternate valid answers](/contributors/alternate-valid-answers).
- Ask whether a knowledgeable student could give a different, equally valid answer. If so, accept it or clarify the stem.

## 4. Inspect the feedback

- Read the rationale as a student. It should explain *why*, including the key distinction from a tempting wrong answer.
- Check that images and other findings appear at the intended time: before answering if required for the decision, after answering if they reveal the solution.
- Check that a citation opens the pages you intended.
- Use the learner view for a full answer attempt. Verify that **Check** or **Submit**, feedback, and reveal all make sense for this item.

## 5. Make the publishing decision

Publish only after the checks above pass. If you need a second reviewer, leave the question as **Draft** and record what remains uncertain outside the student-facing rationale. If the item is outdated but worth retaining, **Archived** removes it from the normal active flow while preserving its record. Review the whole module for duplicates and order in [Reviewing and curating questions](/contributors/reviewing-and-curating-questions).
