---
title: "Writing free-response questions"
description: "Write focused prompts, clear ground truths, and fair acceptance criteria that students can answer in their own words."
---

Use free response when you want students to recall an idea, interpret findings, or explain a decision in their own words. A good prompt makes the expected work clear enough that a student can begin writing without guessing how much detail you want.

## Keep the response manageable

Students may be studying on a tablet or between other tasks. Give them a specific goal and a reasonable answer shape.

- Ask for one decision or explanation at a time.
- State the number of supporting findings or reasons you expect.
- Include the case details needed to answer in the stem or its visible findings.
- Say "in one or two sentences" when a brief answer is sufficient.
- Split a long list of unrelated tasks into separate questions.

The learner editor allows up to 1,500 words. That is a maximum, not a suggested length. Write the prompt so a complete answer can usually be concise.

### Prompt pattern

> Given the findings in this case, name the most likely diagnosis and give one finding that supports it. Answer in one or two sentences.

This tells the student what to decide, what evidence to use, and how much to write. Use the same level of specificity for recall and comparison questions.

## Write the ground truth before publishing

For free response, the required **Rationale** is the ground truth used for grading and shown when the answer is revealed. Write a complete, source-faithful answer that covers every point you ask for in the stem.

Check that the ground truth:

- identifies the main answer and explains the supporting reasoning
- accepts accurate equivalent terms and reasonable paraphrases
- keeps optional detail separate from the points required for acceptance
- contains no requirement that the stem leaves hidden from the student
- matches the case findings, images, and cited source pages

You can attach supporting pages from the Content Library. See [Manual source citations](/contributors/manual-source-citations).

## Set acceptance to match the prompt

The **Acceptance** choice is visible to students. The grader checks meaning and ignores formatting, spelling, and stylistic differences.

| Setting | Use it when |
| --- | --- |
| **Lenient** | The main idea is enough, and minor omissions or imprecise wording are acceptable. This is the default. |
| **Balanced** | The prompt names key points that a complete answer must cover; equivalent wording is welcome. |
| **Strict** | Every requested detail and precise reasoning matter. Make each required detail explicit in the stem. |

Select the least restrictive setting that still tests your objective. A short prompt with a long list of hidden grading requirements makes students unsure how to respond.

## Review the learner flow

Before publishing, open the question as a student would and check the full answer path:

1. Read the stem without the ground truth. Confirm the task and expected depth are clear.
2. Draft a brief accurate response using ordinary wording. Confirm it can be submitted and graded fairly.
3. Review the generated feedback and the comparison under **More**. Revise the stem or ground truth if the feedback expects unstated details.
4. Check the question on a tablet-sized screen. Keep the prompt readable and the response area and **Submit** control usable with the on-screen keyboard.
5. Reveal the answer and confirm the ground truth teaches the point you intended.

Publish when a student can see what to do, answer in a manageable amount of writing, and learn from the feedback.
