---
title: "Writing clinical cases students can reason through"
description: "Build focused, source-supported cases that make a clinical finding necessary to the answer."
---

A clinical case should make the student use patient context or findings to reach one clear decision. Write the decision first; then select the smallest set of details that makes it answerable.

## Build backward from the decision

1. **Name the task.** Decide whether the student will interpret a finding, choose the next assessment step, compare two explanations, or justify a management decision.
2. **Identify the decisive evidence.** List the findings the student must use. Check each against the course source.
3. **Add the context that changes interpretation.** Include age, symptom timing, prior history, or test conditions only when they change the reasoning.
4. **State what to produce.** Ask for one conclusion, or one conclusion plus a specified number of supporting findings.
5. **Write the rationale.** Trace the path from each decisive detail to the answer and address the nearest plausible alternative.

For example, a case about interpreting a clinical test should give the test conditions and results that distinguish the intended interpretations. A stem that merely says a patient “has blurry vision” leaves too many possible answers. A stem with several pages of unrelated history hides the decision. Include enough data to let a student choose and explain an answer without guessing what the author had in mind.

### A reusable case skeleton

> A patient presents with **[relevant complaint and timing]**. **[Finding 1]** and **[finding 2]** were obtained under **[conditions needed to interpret them]**. What is the most likely **[interpretation or next step]**? Give **[one or two]** findings that support your answer.

The bracketed details are planning prompts. Replace them with source-supported facts. If the source does not establish a diagnosis, threshold, or management rule, ask a narrower question that the source does support.

## Make the case do work

Remove each sentence in turn. If the answer remains equally clear, that sentence may be unnecessary. Conversely, if two reasonable answers remain, add the missing discriminator or narrow the task. Avoid irrelevant detail used only to make a question feel difficult.

For multiple choice, make the incorrect options plausible in light of the case. Each should fail for a reason the rationale can explain. For free response, specify the answer shape so students know whether to name a finding, interpret it, or justify a choice. See [Choosing the right question type](/contributors/choosing-question-types).

## Review images and structured findings

If an image or interactive finding is essential, check that it is visible before the student answers. If it is meant to teach after the answer, use solution-only media. Read the case in learner mode and confirm the relevant visual details are legible and the written stem does not disclose the answer. See [Question media and attachments](/contributors/question-media-and-attachments).

Before publishing, have another contributor answer from the stem alone. If they need a fact that exists only in the rationale, add that fact to the stem or change the expected answer.
